# Section videos

What each screen asks the team to fill in is in [screens.md](screens.md). This file is only the five films.



Five short films for one team in What's the problem. The first film sets the cocoa scene. The next four teach the idea, then show the clicks for the step that is about to open. Those four use a different story, a missing football at break, so the team does not copy the cocoa answers into the board.

The films are not recorded yet. The room already has the player, the teacher controls, and the read-through. A dark frame that says “Video will play here” stands in for the file.

## When the teacher plays each film

The teacher plays the film out loud to the whole team, from the teacher screen, and only then presses Start or Next. Student laptops do not autoplay. Five speakers at once would make the room unusable. A student who missed the film, or who wants the steps again, opens **Read this step** on their own screen. That page is the same teaching, in writing, and it stays available after the film has played.

| Film | Teacher is looking at | Plays, then presses | Students arrive on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The chocolate bar | Welcome (`s0`) | Start is still waiting. Play film 2 next. | Still the welcome |
| 2. Stakeholders | Welcome (`s0`), after film 1 | **Start** | Who is touched by this? |
| 3. The incident | Power map (`s2`), when the person has been picked | **Next** | Pin one real incident |
| 4. The fish | Incident (`s3`), when one incident is chosen | **Next** | Map possible causes on the fish |
| 5. The statement | Pins (`s4d`), when the pin is on the fish | **Next** | Write one five-blank problem statement each |

Films 2, 4, and 5 each cover more than one screen. The film plays once, at the gate above. When the teacher later presses Next inside that stretch, there is no second film. The read-through is split by screen so the team can scroll to the part that matches the board.

Suggested length: film 1 about two minutes, films 2, 3, and 5 about three minutes, film 4 about four minutes.

## Where it sits on screen

On the teacher view, the player sits in **Class overview**, above the Start / Next button for the gate in the table. The heading is the film title. Under the player: **Read this step**, the same text the students see. A checkbox, **The class has seen this**, sits beside Start or Next. The button still works if the film will not load. The checkbox is a reminder, not a lock.

On the student board, under the five phase tiles, a line reads **Read this step**. It opens the written version for the stretch they are in. **Watch again** is on that same panel and starts only when a student presses it.

The five read-throughs stay in a short list so a student can return to an earlier film. The one for the current stretch is open.

| Stretch | Screens | Read-through parts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scene | Welcome | Film 1 |
| Stakeholders | People, Merge, Power | Film 2, three parts |
| Incident | Incident | Film 3 |
| Fish | Causes, Choose a bone, Why, Pin | Film 4, four parts |
| Statement | Statement, Choose 3, then the pack | Film 5, two parts |

## Roles, once

Say the jobs, not the sample names. The sample room uses Alex, Leo, Maya, Asha, and Kai. Any class will have other names.

| Job | What the films should call them |
| --- | --- |
| Leader | Keeps the pace. Locks a step when the board is ready. |
| Lead Interviewer | Stays with the real person. The only student who types a question to the interview persona. |
| Theme Mapper | Merges duplicate people, places them on the matrix, and places the pin on the fish. |
| Insight Extractor | Asks Why, marks the root cause, and writes the two pin labels. |
| Devil's Advocate | Stops blame, vague cards, bad merges, and solutions hiding inside a problem. |

The teacher moves the screens and runs the clock. Students do not press Next.

## The football, used in films 2 to 5

One toy story, so the four tool films feel like one lesson.

A student brings a football for break. On Thursday the whistle goes and the ball is still in the shed. The game never starts.

People worth naming: the student who brought the ball, the year group waiting to play, the duty teacher, the coach, and the caretaker who locks the shed.

The team follows the student who brought the ball.

One incident: "On Thursday at outdoor break, the ball was still in the shed when the whistle went, and the game never started."

One cause, on the Tools rib: "There is one key, and it leaves with the caretaker."

The root Why, often the third answer rather than a forced fifth: "The lock-up round is scheduled before break, so the key is off the site when the game should start."

One statement: "The student who brought the ball needs to get a game going before break ends, but there is one key and it leaves with the caretaker, so on Thursday the ball was still in the shed when the whistle went, because the lock-up round is scheduled before break."

## Film 1 — The chocolate bar

**Job.** Set the scene. No clicks. After this film the team knows the theme is the hidden world behind a chocolate bar, and that their job is to find the problem rather than invent a fix.

**Play on.** Welcome, before film 2, before Start.

### What to say

Open on an ordinary chocolate bar. Most of the people who will eat it never see the farm.

The work happens on cocoa farms, including farms in Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana. Family workers cut the pods, ferment the beans, and dry them. Children are often part of that work. The tools are machetes and the buyer's scale. Beans dry in the open, so rain can spoil a week of work. Buying agents, cooperatives, and chocolate makers all stand between the farm and the bar.

Then the number the class will keep hearing: the farmer receives about 6 cents of each dollar spent on the bar.

Close on the question this room answers. Who is this a problem for? What happens to them? Why does it keep happening? The team will not design a new bar, an app, or a campaign today. They will leave with three clear problem statements.

### On screen

The bar, then a simple chain: farm, buying station, maker, shop. One card for the 6 cents. End on the room's phase tiles, with no instruction to click yet.

### Read this step

A chocolate bar hides the people who made it. On cocoa farms, families cut the pods, ferment the beans, and dry them. Children often do part of that work. Machetes, spray, and a scale the family does not control are part of the day. Rain can spoil beans that are drying outside. Buying agents, cooperatives, and chocolate makers sit between the farm and the shop.

The farmer receives about 6 cents of each dollar spent on the bar.

In this room you find the problem. You do not design a solution. You will name who it is for, find one real moment, trace why that moment happens, and write three problem statements the team can stand behind.

## Film 2 — Who is in this, and who do we follow?

**Job.** Teach stakeholder mapping, then the clicks from the silent pile through the matrix, ending when one person is chosen.

**Play on.** Welcome, after film 1. The teacher presses Start only after this film.

**Screens it covers.** Who is touched by this? (`s1`, about 4 minutes, silent). Clean the pile (`s1b`, about 3 minutes). Who has power? Who cares? Whose problem do we follow? (`s2`). There is no vote on the person. The Lead Interviewer picks.

### What to say

A stakeholder is a person or a group the situation touches. Name a kind of person, such as "caretaker" or "year group". Do not name a product, an app, or a solution.

Show the football, not the farm. Five cards go on the table: the student who brought the ball, the year group, the duty teacher, the coach, the caretaker. Two cards that mean the same person get stacked. "The boy with the ball" and "the student who brought the football" are one person. The duty teacher and the coach are not the same person, even if both are adults. The Theme Mapper merges. Everyone else can point at a pair. The Devil's Advocate can stop a merge that glues two different people together.

Then the matrix. Power is how much they can change what happens. Interest is how much they care. Place each card:

|  | Low interest | High interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High power | Keep satisfied. The caretaker has the key and may not care about the game. | Work closely. The coach wants the game and can change the routine. |
| Low power | Monitor. | Keep involved. The student who brought the ball cares a lot and cannot open the shed. |

The Lead Interviewer taps one person to follow and writes what goes wrong for them. In the film, that person is the student who brought the ball, and what goes wrong is that break starts and there is no ball. Say clearly that this sentence is an early guess. The checked moment comes in the next film. There is no class vote on this choice.

Point at the jobs in the app, in order:

1. Everyone adds people in silence. Aim for three cards each. Others cannot see them until the teacher ends the time.
2. The Theme Mapper drags one card onto its twin. Others tap Suggest same. The Mapper taps Merges done.
3. The Theme Mapper places every remaining card on the matrix.
4. The Lead Interviewer taps the one person the team will follow, marks whether the team has heard from them or is guessing, and writes what goes wrong.
5. The Insight Extractor can star someone who feels this every day. The Devil's Advocate flags a missing person who could block the situation, and challenges a card that is only a guess.

Then the teacher presses Next and the incident screen opens. Do not press it inside this film.

### Read this step

**People.** Add the people this theme touches. Use a short label for a person or a group. Leave products and solutions off the board. Work in silence until the teacher ends the time. Three cards each is the aim. Fewer cards do not stop the room.

**Merge.** Stack cards that name the same person. Do not retype them. Only the Theme Mapper merges, by dragging one card onto the other. Anyone else can suggest a pair. The Devil's Advocate can stop one bad merge. When the pile is clean, the Theme Mapper taps Merges done.

**Matrix.** The Theme Mapper places every card. High or low power means how much they can change what happens. High or low interest means how much they care. The four corners are Work closely, Keep satisfied, Keep involved, and Monitor.

The Lead Interviewer taps the one person the team will follow and writes what goes wrong for that person. That sentence is the early guess. There is no vote. The Insight Extractor can mark daily pain. The Devil's Advocate looks for a missing blocker and for guesses that are being treated as facts.

In the football example, the team follows the student who brought the ball. The caretaker has the key and may not care about the game. The student cares and cannot open the shed.

## Film 3 — Talk to them, then pick one moment

**Job.** Show why a short interview matters, how the interview works in this room, and how the team turns that conversation into one incident. That incident is the core problem the fish will carry in its head.

**Play on.** The power map, after the person is chosen, before Next.

**Screen it covers.** Pin one real incident (`s3`).

### What to say

You have a person. You do not yet have a moment. A stakeholder interview is how you stop guessing about their day. You ask what they are trying to get done, what gets in the way, and when and where it happened. You are listening for a fact, not for their advice on a solution.

The room already holds a short version of that interview, so the team can move in one lesson. It is the team asking, and that person answering. The person speaks as someone who does this work. They use real seasons, prices, and what actually went wrong. It is not a recording of one visit.

Show the football interview in four beats:

- What are you trying to get done? "I am trying to get a game going before break ends."
- What gets in the way? "The ball is locked in the shed."
- One moment. "On Thursday, at outdoor break, the whistle went and the ball was still inside."
- What do you wish people understood? "I brought the ball. I still could not start the game."

Then the tool. The short interview appears for everyone. Under it, only the Lead Interviewer has a box. That person may ask up to eight more questions. Everyone else reads the questions and the answers as they arrive. They do not each open their own chat. Extra questions stay on the person's day. If someone asks "what app should we build?", the persona refuses to pick a solution.

After the team has a moment, they write it as an incident. The shape is when, where, and what happened. "On Thursday at outdoor break, the ball was still in the shed when the whistle went, and the game never started." That is a fact. "People are careless with equipment" is blame, and the Devil's Advocate can send it back.

The Lead Interviewer usually writes the first incident. Others may add a different moment. The team votes. The Leader locks the winner, or the teacher pressing Next locks the one with the most votes. The winner becomes the head of the fish. It is the core problem the team will explain. It is not a cause, and it is not a solution.

### Read this step

The person you picked is on the board. The short interview is a faster version of a longer conversation. The team asks. That person answers as someone who does the work, with real seasons, prices, and what went wrong. Read what they are trying to get done, what gets in the way, and one moment with a when and a where.

Only the Lead Interviewer types extra questions. Everyone else watches the answers appear. Eight questions is the limit. Ask about their day. Do not ask them to choose your solution.

Then write one incident: a fact that happened, with a when, a where, and a what. "On Thursday at outdoor break, the ball was still in the shed when the whistle went" is an incident. "They are careless" is not. The Devil's Advocate can reject blame.

Vote for one incident. The winner is the core problem. It goes in the head of the fish on the next screen.

## Film 4 — The fish, one rib, the root, the pin

**Job.** Show the incident becoming the fish head, the six angles a cause can come from, the choice of one rib, the Why chain, and the pin.

**Play on.** The incident screen, after the winning incident is clear, before Next.

**Screens it covers.** Map possible causes (`s4a`). Pick the one bone (`s4b`). Ask Why (`s4c`). Pin (`s4d`).

### What to say

The head of the fish is the incident you just chose. The rest of the fish asks why that moment happened. It does not ask how to fix it.

Six ribs, six angles:

| Rib | The question it asks |
| --- | --- |
| Timing | When did it happen, or how long did it take? |
| Communication | What was someone told, or not told? |
| Perceived value | Did it feel worth the effort? |
| Process | Which steps did people have to follow? |
| Tools | What did people have to use? |
| Environment | Where was it, and what was around it? |

On the football fish the head reads: the ball was still in the shed when the whistle went. One cause lands on each rib. Timing: break is only fifteen minutes. Communication: nobody is told the shed will lock. Perceived value: a short game feels pointless. Process: the shed is locked on a round that finishes before break. Tools: there is one key, and it leaves with the caretaker. Environment: the shed is across the field.

In the app, each person picks a rib, writes one cause of the incident, and adds it. The card appears on that rib. The Theme Mapper can move a card that landed on the wrong rib. The Devil's Advocate stamps a card that blames a person or says nothing you could check. Nobody picks the root yet.

Next, the team reads the six ribs and taps **one** rib to follow. The football team taps Tools. The five Whys happen on that rib only.

The Insight Extractor asks Why, and the answer grows to the left. Why is there one key that leaves with the caretaker? Because every key is on one ring. Why does the ring leave? Because it goes on the lock-up round. Why is the key gone at break? Because the lock-up round is scheduled before break. Stop there. The third Why is the root. A fourth or a fifth is allowed, and so is stopping earlier, when the answer is the reason you trust. Tap that card and mark **This Why is the root cause**. Each Why is tagged Evidence, Assumption, or Need to check. The Lead Interviewer can name who to ask on a Need-to-check card. Other students may propose a Why. Only the Insight Extractor adds it to the rib.

The pin carries two labels into the statement, and they are not the same card:

- Obstacle is the cause written on the rib. In the film: "There is one key, and it leaves with the caretaker."
- Insight is the Why marked as the root. In the film: "The lock-up round is scheduled before break."

The first Why is an explanation of the cause. It does not replace the cause. The Insight Extractor checks those two labels. The Theme Mapper places the pin on the fish. One to three pins are allowed. The incident in the head is not one of these labels. It becomes Harm on the next screen.

### Read this step

**Causes.** The incident is the head of the fish. Each rib is one kind of cause: Timing, Communication, Perceived value, Process, Tools, Environment. Pick a rib, write why the incident happened, and add the card. The Theme Mapper moves a card that is on the wrong rib. Do not choose a root yet, and do not write a solution.

**One rib.** Read the cause on each rib. Tap the one rib the team will follow. The Whys grow on that rib only.

**Why.** Only the Insight Extractor adds a Why. Each answer sits further left. Tag it Evidence, Assumption, or Need to check. Stop when one answer is the root cause. That is often the third or the fourth Why. Tap it and mark it. You do not have to write a fifth.

**Pin.** The Insight Extractor checks two labels. Obstacle is the cause on the rib. Insight is the Why marked as the root. The Theme Mapper places the pin. The incident stays in the head. It is not the obstacle and it is not the insight.

In the football example the rib is Tools. The obstacle is the single key that leaves with the caretaker. The root is that lock-up happens before break.

## Film 5 — One sentence, then three keepers

**Job.** Teach the problem-statement sentence, where each blank comes from, and how five sentences become three.

**Play on.** The pin screen, after the pin is placed, before Next.

**Screens it covers.** Write one statement (`s5`, silent, about 7 minutes). Clean the statements and pick the top 3 (`s6`). The pack (`s7`) needs no film. The read-through tells the team the three sentences are what they take with them.

### What to say

The sentence has five blanks:

**[who] needs [need], but [obstacle], so [harm], because [insight].**

Say where each word comes from, using the football, then point at the same blanks on the cocoa board. The sources are the same. The words will be different.

| Blank | Comes from | Football |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | The person the Lead Interviewer followed | The student who brought the ball |
| Need | The interview answer to "what are you trying to get done?", edited by the student | to get a game going before break ends |
| Obstacle | The cause on the rib that was pinned | there is one key, and it leaves with the caretaker |
| Harm | The incident in the fish head | on Thursday the ball was still in the shed when the whistle went |
| Insight | The Why marked as the root | the lock-up round is scheduled before break |

Need is the only blank the student shapes. It is a human need. "An app that unlocks the shed" is a solution, and the board will not accept it. The other blanks arrive filled in. Edit one if it is wrong. An edit is kept. It is not overwritten when the map changes.

Each person writes their own sentence and submits it. The room waits until all five are in. Writing is silent.

On the next screen the team checks the five sentences. The Devil's Advocate can tag a sentence that is too broad, that contains two problems, that smuggles in a solution, that says "users" or "people" instead of the person, or that lost its obstacle or its harm. The Insight Extractor may propose a fix. Only the author accepts it. The Theme Mapper stacks two sentences that say the same thing, without retyping them. The Lead Interviewer asks whether the person would recognise themselves.

The Leader opens the vote. Each student has two votes. The top three sentences stay. The Leader locks the pack. Those three, plus the map and the fish, are what the team takes out of the room.

### Read this step

**Write.** The sentence is: who needs need, but obstacle, so harm, because insight.

Who is the person you followed. Need starts from the interview line about what they are trying to get done. Edit it so it is a human need, not a product. Obstacle is the cause you pinned. Harm is the incident in the fish head. Insight is the Why marked as the root cause. Change a blank that is wrong. Submit your own sentence, then wait for the other four.

**Choose 3.** The Devil's Advocate tags weak sentences. The Insight Extractor can propose a fix. You accept a fix only on your own card. The Theme Mapper stacks duplicates. When the Leader opens the vote, you have two votes. The team keeps three sentences.

The football sentence, so you can see the shape: "The student who brought the ball needs to get a game going before break ends, but there is one key and it leaves with the caretaker, so on Thursday the ball was still in the shed when the whistle went, because the lock-up round is scheduled before break."

Your sentences are about the chocolate bar, not about the football.

## What the films should not teach

These are older versions of the board. A film that says them will fight the screen.

- The interview is asked by the team, not by "the class". Only the Lead Interviewer types in the box underneath.
- The person on the matrix is chosen by the Lead Interviewer. The room does not vote for that person.
- The team follows one rib, not two or three.
- Obstacle is the cause on that rib. It is not the first Why.
- Harm in the sentence is the incident in the fish head. "What goes wrong" on the matrix was the earlier guess.
- Insight is the Why that was marked as the root. It is not automatically the longest chain.
- The phase tile for the sentence is **Define**. It is not "Test ideas". This room does not test solutions.
